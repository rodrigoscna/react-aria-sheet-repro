Sheet stays entering, off-screen, when its arrival scroll is interrupted

react-aria-components@1.22.1, react/react-dom@19.3.0, Chromium. A side Sheet enters by smooth-scrolling its [data-sheet-scroll] container, and onEnter resolves only on that container's next scrollend. If a button inside the sheet takes focus early in that scroll, the focus's own scroll-into-view stops it short, and the sheet is left open with its panel off the window's right edge and its scrim over the page, either still data-entering or done entering at the exit position. Issue: adobe/react-spectrum#11015.

The setup: <SheetOverlay position="right" isOpen> mounted fresh, as on a page load, with a MenuTrigger button inside it. Sheet is a public export (import {SheetOverlay, Sheet, SheetContent} from 'react-aria-components', as src/main.jsx does, or from 'react-aria-components/Sheet'), implemented in dist/private/Sheet.mjs. UNSTABLE_deferUntilEntered and UNSTABLE_overrideFocus are not props you pass: SheetOverlay always sets both on its ModalOverlay.

The sheet opens with the page. While it is open the page behind it is inert, so close it first (Esc, or its Close button) before using these.

Manual: press Reopen, then press Tab then Enter while the sheet slides in. The window is the first ~150 ms of a ~640 ms arrival: a Tab landing later than about 200 ms after Reopen lets the sheet arrive normally. It is easiest from the keyboard: Tab to Reopen, then press Enter and Tab in one quick roll. CPU throttling in DevTools (Performance panel → CPU, 4x to 6x) does not widen it in Chromium, since the smooth scroll runs on a clock rather than on the main thread; measured with 6x throttling, the arrival still takes ~630 ms.

Reproduce does it in one click: it reopens the sheet and, in the first frame of the arrival, focuses the menu button and presses Enter on it the way a keyboard does.

What interrupts it is the focus(), because it scrolls the scroller to bring the button into view; the press only opens the menu. A focus({preventScroll: true}) followed by the same Enter leaves the arrival alone. Focus in the very first frame leaves the sheet data-entering for good, since no scrollend follows. Focus a little later, before about 200 ms, ends with the scroller back at 0, its exited position, and a scrollend that resolves the enter there, so data-entering clears while the panel stays entirely off-screen. The sheet's IntersectionObserver never saw the panel, so it does not close either.
